# PROJECT.md — Emerging Disease Forecasting Framework

**A Generalizable Spatio-Temporal Framework for Emerging Infectious Disease Forecasting**
Transferable representations · few-shot adaptation · calibrated uncertainty · explanation

**Purpose of this file.** A single, current source of truth for what the project is, what has
been built through Phase 2, how each piece feeds the final journal submission, and what Phase 3
must still do. It is written to seed the Phase-3 plan. Status as of 2026-07-20.

**Target outcome.** A reproducible, novel methodological contribution suitable for a high-impact
(ISI-indexed) computational-epidemiology / applied-ML journal — code plus manuscript.

---

## 1. The thesis and the gap

An emerging pathogen leaves almost no history at the moment a forecast matters most. Rather than
learn from one disease's own long record, **learn transferable spatio-temporal structure from
data-rich diseases (dengue, influenza) and carry it, few-shot, to a data-scarce one (Ebola)** —
with calibrated uncertainty and faithful explanation.

The competitive analysis across ten representative spatio-temporal GNNs (manuscript §2–§4)
locates a precise, empty intersection: **no incumbent is simultaneously (a) disease-agnostic by
construction, (b) transferable across diseases, (c) few-shot adaptable to a genuinely unseen
pathogen, and (d) calibrated and explained.** Mechanistic hybrids (CausalGNN, MepoGNN, HeatGNN,
STOEP, BDGSTN) are single-disease and uncalibrated; attention/risk models (Cola-GNN, EpiGNN) fit
each disease separately and give point forecasts; the closest multi-disease system (Liu & Cao
2026) is joint multi-task over a fixed panel with only a leave-one-disease-out curve, not few-shot
on an unseen pathogen. **Occupying that intersection is the contribution.**

## 3. Phase 1 — Foundations (complete)

Delivered the groundwork a comparative model is built on and judged against. Captured in the
Phase-1 manuscript ([Emerging_Disease_Forecasting_Manuscript.docx](Emerging_Disease_Forecasting_Manuscript.docx))
and the Reports/ set.

- **Formalisation** — emerging-disease forecasting as multi-horizon, joint multi-node incidence
  prediction over one shared representation; one shared encoder with light per-disease adaptation
  (the shared-parameter formulation is what underwrites the transfer claim). Horizons
  h ∈ {3, 5, 10, 15}, lookback w = 20, direct multi-horizon (no autoregressive rollout).
- **Standardised input schema** — the `DiseaseTensors` contract (`X, A_geo, A_mob, C, M, y, meta`),
  a **core transfer-safe block of exactly 4 channels** (normalised incidence, sin/cos day-of-year,
  observation mask) and an excluded extended block. Spec in [schema_spec.md](schema_spec.md),
  loaders in [to_schema.py](to_schema.py).
- **Evaluation protocol** (frozen before any comparative run) — chronological 50/20/30 + rolling
  origin; RMSE/MAE/PCC + sMAPE, peak-intensity/timing error; CRPS/PICP/interval-width for UQ;
  5 seeds {42,52,62,72,82}; Diebold–Mariano / Friedman–Nemenyi significance battery; LODO
  transfer + few-shot k-shot sweep.
- **Competitive analysis** — ten models scored on the design axes; the empty intersection is the
  documented gap (manuscript Table 1, §3–§4).
- **Baseline reproduction** — 8 SOTA models run on their own shipped data (see §7 below).

## 4. Phase 2 — Multi-disease data engineering (complete)

Three separately-collected surveillance datasets turned into **five harmonised, leakage-clean
bundles** that one model reads without being told which disease it is. Regenerated by a single
command ([build_datasets.py](build_datasets.py)), gated by 86 correctness checks. Full record in
[data_audit.md](data_audit.md); reproducibility in [data_pipeline.md](data_pipeline.md).

| Dataset | Role | Nodes | Weeks | Observed cells | Split |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dengue (12 countries) | development | 7,165 | 1,409 (1998–2024) | 2,196,261 | per-country chronological 50/20/30 |
| Influenza — Japan | development | 47 | 348 | 16,356 | fixed 50/20/30 |
| Influenza — US regions | development | 10 | 785 | 7,850 | fixed 50/20/30 |
| Influenza — US states | development | 49 | 360 | 17,640 | fixed 50/20/30 |
| Ebola (GIN/LBR/SLE) | **few-shot holdout** | 61 | 52 (2014–2015) | 1,299 | calendar-prefix support/query |

**What makes it publication-grade — the quality spine:**

- **Leakage suite gates the build.** 86 pass/fail gates + **6 negative controls** that plant a
  real defect and require the matching gate to fail; nothing is written if any gate fails
  ([test_leakage.py](tests/test_leakage.py)). A gate that cannot fail proves nothing.
- **Adversarial review caught two invalidating defects that passed 83 green gates** — the Ebola
  cumulative→weekly clip fabricated **35.8% of the target** (fixed: running-maximum envelope,
  mass-conserving, verified 24,552 = reference), and a dengue split fallback put **31% of observed
  cells in mixed train/test columns** (fixed: per-country boundary, no fallback). Both are now
  gated (`gate_mass_conservation`, `gate_phase_purity`).
- **Provenance & reproducibility.** All 18 raw inputs checksummed before the build; GADM 4.1
  fetched-by-manifest (`fetch_gadm.py`); deterministic (rebuild-and-rehash); packaged as `.npz`
  (plain arrays + JSON meta), never pickle.
- **Every data alteration declared, reasoned, reversible, recorded in metadata** (dengue J1–J11;
  Ebola's drops) — this register is the paper's data-methods section.

**Disease-agnosticism enforced in code, not discipline.** `transfer_view()` exposes only the 4
core channels; deaths, static covariates, and self-loop conventions are withheld from the shared
encoder because each would let the encoder infer *which* disease it is looking at (disjoint
geography makes a raw centroid a disease tell).

## 5. This session's close-outs (2026-07-20)

- **Ebola validation protocol — decided.** All hyperparameters selected on the development
  diseases (encoder settings on dev val folds; adaptation settings by leave-one-development-
  disease-out), then frozen and applied to Ebola once; Ebola's query set scored a single time,
  fixed adaptation steps (no early-stop on query); uncertainty via online adaptive conformal
  inference (no held-out calibration set). Ebola carries **no** validation split by design.
- **Japan influenza calendar — pinned to the week.** Was the weakest anchor (seasonal phase only,
  ±4 wk). Now identified against NIID's dated season peaks: 2018/19 → 2019-01-26 (week 4 of 2019),
  2017/18 → 2018-02-03 (week 5 of 2018), both exact; guarded by
  [japan_calendar_pin.py](japan_calendar_pin.py). The two US calendars were already cell-identified
  against CDC ILINet — all three influenza calendars are now source-identified.
- **Country-macro scoring metric — implemented.** [score.py](score.py): per node → mean within
  country → mean across the 12 countries equally, neutralising Brazil's 77% node share; self-test
  shows a Bolivia-only error moves the node mean 0.015 but the country-macro 0.833 (≈10/12).
- **Five pending audit decisions — closed** and written into [data_audit.md](data_audit.md) §5.4:
  constant-node scoring (exclude from scoring, keep in graph, Japan sensitivity); new-cases series
  adopted as a cross-check; **Brazilian dominance — uniform sampling primary, balanced sampler as
  an ablation** (decided empirically); support window (calendar cutoff 2014-05-24 primary, variable
  window as a parameter); dengue finest-available resolution approved (A1). **No blockers remain.**
- **Manuscript reconciliation memo** — [manuscript_reconciliation.md](manuscript_reconciliation.md):
  exact old→new edits for every passage Phase 2 invalidated (critically, eq. 4, still the retracted
  clip formula).

## 8. Phase 3 — what remains (Weeks 3–6, seeds the plan)

The foundation is complete and unblocked. Phase 3 is the modelling contribution.

**Week 3 — Core framework + development training (G1, G2, G6).**
- Implement the disease-agnostic spatio-temporal encoder (temporal + graph) on the 4-core-channel
  `transfer_view`. **Honour the three coded constraints** (data_audit §4.6): the encoder must add
  the identity before degree normalisation (4 isolated influenza nodes divide by zero otherwise);
  static covariates `C` must not reach the shared encoder; the scaler must be refit at every
  rolling origin (`raw` is shipped for this).
- Optional epidemiology-informed component (metapopulation/compartmental-aware or PINN loss) —
  brief [SUGGESTION].
- **Build the country-macro scorer into the training/eval loop** ([score.py](score.py)); build a
  **country-balanced sampler as an ablation** vs the uniform-sampling primary.
- Re-run trusted baselines under the common pipeline (identical data/splits/horizons/metrics/seeds).

**Week 4 — Transfer, meta-learning, few-shot (G2, G3).**
- Meta-learning / domain-generalisation training (MAML-style) for disease-invariant representations.
- Leave-one-disease-out transfer across development diseases (zero-shot + few-shot).
- Few-shot adaptation protocol for Ebola, per the **decided validation protocol** (§5): select on
  development LODO, freeze, apply to Ebola once; k-shot sweep.

**Week 5 — Ebola case study, uncertainty, explainability (G3, G4, G5).**
- Run the Ebola out-of-distribution few-shot case study (52 of 61 districts are zero-shot — the
  genuine emerging-outbreak regime).
- Calibrated uncertainty via **adaptive conformal inference** (online; no held-out calibration
  set) + CRPS/PICP/width coverage diagnostics.
- SHAP global + local explanations.

**Week 6 — Ablations, robustness, writing (G7).**
- Ablations: with/without epidemiology-informed component; standard vs meta-learning; **uniform vs
  country-balanced sampling**; constant-node-included vs excluded (Japan sensitivity).
- Robustness/sensitivity (rolling-origin), significance battery, final tables/figures.
- Apply the manuscript reconciliation; draft the full paper; finalise the reproducibility package.

**Cross-cutting adoptions decided this session:** country-macro as the dengue headline;
new-cases as an Ebola cross-check series; Ebola support cutoff 2014-05-24 primary with a variable
window as an ablation for comparability.

## 9. Open risks / watch items for Phase 3

- **Manuscript–dataset drift** — eq. 4 still prints the retracted Ebola clip; apply the
  reconciliation memo early so it isn't quoted anywhere.
- **Ebola is tiny and has no validation set** — protecting the single-shot query evaluation is the
  sharpest correctness risk; keep all selection on the development diseases.
- **Baseline comparability** — horizon/metric standardisation across the suite is the top threat to
  any SOTA claim (G6).
- **Transfer through disjoint geography** — `C` and any geographic feature must stay out of the
  shared encoder; the diseases occupy disjoint regions, so geography identifies the disease.
- **Schedule** — the entire novel contribution (Weeks 3–6) is still ahead and is the densest part;
  the data is no longer the risk, the modelling clock is.
- **Traceability housekeeping** — the released `.npz` were built from an **uncommitted** working
  tree; commit `to_schema.py`, `test_leakage.py`, `build_datasets.py`, the audit, `score.py`,
  `japan_calendar_pin.py`, `manuscript_reconciliation.md`, and `data/processed/` before modelling.
