HEADLINE
--------
* Single-disease encoder: clean win over naive floors on US-STATES only.
  Mixed on the other three (loses short-horizon on dengue, loses to seasonal
  on Japan). Real forecaster, not a uniform slam-dunk vs trivial baselines.
* Multi-disease JOINT training: helps NOTHING, hurts the two SMALLEST datasets
  (Japan, US-regions) -- the Ebola-scale regime. => NEGATIVE TRANSFER on the
  regime the thesis targets. This is the critical result.

   +help% = joint better ; -help% = joint WORSE
================================================================================
                          RMSE      MAE      PCC     NET
  dengue (7165 nodes)     -0.4%    +0.3%    -3.1%    flat  (dengue dominates trunk)
  us-states (49 nodes)    +0.1%    -0.6%    +0.8%    flat
  us-regions (10 nodes)   -4.9%    -7.3%   -10.2%    HURT
  japan (47 nodes)        -8.0%    -6.9%    -3.1%    HURT
  worst cells: japan h15 RMSE -18% ; us-regions h15 PCC -21%
  No MATERIAL/consistent gain anywhere: only scattered sub-3% wins on the two
  flat datasets (e.g. dengue MAE h10 +2.4%), none stable across metrics.

  READ:
  - dengue flat = it is 98.5% of nodes, joint ~ single by construction (not a test).
  - the SMALL diseases (the real test) got WORSE -> negative transfer.
  - the datasets hurt most (10, 47 nodes) are the Ebola-scale ones (Ebola=61).
  - TWO explanations, undistinguished by this run:
      (a) genuine negative transfer (different disease dynamics fight one trunk)
      (b) weighting artifact (uniform over-cycles tiny data ~24x -> overfit)
    -> resolvable cheaply: re-run sqrt/proportional weighting + LODO probe.
