## 1. Why the spatial graph does not help

We already knew it does not help. The gate-off ablation showed the spatial channel helps error in
**0 of 40** cells and hurts in 8 (`results/reports/gate_ablation.log:134`, which tallies 60 cells
over 25 runs, 40 error plus 20 correlation). That told us the fact. It did not tell us the reason.

I found the reason. Four steps, each measured.

**Step 1. The model does use the graph.** It is not quietly switched off. From
`Reports/gated+spatial_Contribution.txt`, the gate mean runs 0.271 to 0.604 across panels and the
spatial contribution runs 0.395 to 0.637. **0.0 percent** of nodes sit below g=0.05. So the model
opens the gate wide and pushes a lot of signal through it.

**Step 2. Every district's summary is mostly the same summary.** Each district gets 64 numbers out of
the encoder. Between 78 and 96 percent of those numbers are a shared component that is identical
across districts. The share that is actually district-specific:

| panel | district-specific share |
|---|---|
| influenza_japan | 3.8% |
| covid_us-states | 4.8% |
| influenza_us-regions | 8.6% |
| influenza_us-states | 12.3% |
| dengue | 22.4% |

The personal part lives in about **2 of the 64 dimensions**. In plain words: the model can tell
districts apart, but only barely, and it is using a 64-slot space as roughly a 2-slot space.

**Step 3. Averaging over neighbours deletes most of what little is personal.** The neighbour average
destroys 14 to 60 percent of the district-specific content. The reason is simple. If everyone's
vector is mostly the same vector plus a small personal wobble, averaging a handful of them cancels
the wobbles and leaves the shared part.

**Step 4. So the graph's identity barely matters.** I relabelled the adjacency at inference time,
keeping the exact topology and every node's degree, but attaching the wrong districts to it. The cost:

| panel | error cost of scrambling the graph |
|---|---|
| influenza_japan | +0.04% |
| covid_us-states | +0.26% |
| dengue | +0.59% |
| influenza_us-states | +0.74% |
| influenza_us-regions | +1.04% |

Seed noise is 8 to 14 percent. So knowing the true neighbours is worth about **one tenth of noise**.

The relabelling displacement equals the full size of the personal component (ratios 1.03 to 1.14
across all five panels). So it scrambles essentially everything personal in the neighbour message,
and still costs under 1 percent.

**The honest reading.** The real graph IS better than a fake one. The sign is correct on all five
panel means. It is worth about half a percent.

**Downsides and limits, stated.**
- 3 of the 20 individual seed cells came out negative: influenza_japan seed 42 at -0.09%, covid seed
  52 at -0.02%, covid seed 72 at -0.19%.
- These use `results/single/` checkpoints, not the transfer trunk.
- Dengue is one seed and five permutations. The other four panels are five seeds and twenty
  permutations each.
- This is **not** the retrain-based shuffled-adjacency control still listed as optional. It answers
  the cheaper question of whether the *trained* model uses the real graph.

## 2. Is mean averaging the problem?

I measured what other aggregators could see on the same trained features. Reproduce with
`diagnostics/graph_probe/t4.py`.

```
panel                    h    mean     sum     max  spread | nbr disagree
influenza_japan        3.8%   3.3%   17.1%   2.9%   25.7% |   0.108
influenza_us-regions   8.6%   3.5%   13.2%   3.2%   24.8% |   0.202
influenza_us-states   12.3%   5.0%   16.4%   8.8%   26.7% |   0.258
covid_us-states        4.8%   3.9%   15.3%   3.5%   24.5% |   0.121
```

- Mean averaging is a **choice**, not something a GNN is forced into.
- `max` collapses as badly as mean. Swapping to it buys nothing.
- `sum` looks 3 to 5 times better, but the gain is not real. `sum_i = deg_i * mean_i`, so it differs
  from the mean only by a per-node degree scalar, and LTR already feeds degree into the model at
  `models/encoder.py:60`. Redundant with code we already have.
- Neighbour **spread**, meaning max minus min across neighbours, is 24.5 to 26.7 percent
  district-specific. That is **higher than the node's own vector**. Averaging deletes it by
  construction, because "my neighbours all agree" and "my neighbours disagree wildly" produce the
  same average.
- Neighbours do disagree with each other, by 11 to 26 percent of their own size. So there is real
  variation being thrown away.

**Two caveats, and they are not footnotes.**
- Distinct is not the same as useful. A channel with a lot of variation can be pure noise. This
  measures information that is *available*, not information that *helps forecasting*. It is a
  hypothesis, nothing more.
- Any aggregator change is a **retrain**, roughly LDO3 trunk cost. Not an inference-time tweak. And
  it does not touch the upstream problem, which is that the encoder only uses about 2 of its 64
  dimensions.

## 4. A claim I made and withdrew

I said the Ebola adapter has roughly **50 parameters per observation**, and that over-parameterisation
is why few-shot adaptation makes things worse.

That does not reproduce. From `configs/ebola_arms.json`:

| arm | support cells | adaptation pairs | params per cell | params per pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ebola_L12 | 59 | 104 | 24 | 14 |
| ebola_L20 | 113 | 320 | 13 | 4.5 |

No denominator gives 50.

The story also fails on its own logic. L20 has **more** data per parameter, so it should be hurt
**less**. It is hurt far more: 16 of 16 cells, worst case MAE h15 going from 21.790 to 31.541.

**The explanation is withdrawn. There is no established replacement.**

What is still solid: zero-shot beats few-shot in **31 of 32** cells on point means, L12 15 of 16 and
L20 16 of 16.

**And its fence.** `Reports/Phase0_to_Now_Audit.md:256` puts "zero-shot beats few-shot" on the
unsafe-to-claim list **as a tested result**, because the paired-by-seed test clears in only 4 of 16
cells. My 31 of 32 has no confidence interval. It strengthens an **observation**. It does not make it
a test. It is also a different statistic from the "unadapted wins 12 of 16" already on record, which
counts significant wins against persistence. Do not let the three get merged.

## 5. Cross-disease transfer against the published baselines

Until today the baselines had only ever been compared against our **single-disease** encoder. The
LDO3 transfer numbers had only ever been compared against our own single-disease ceiling. The two
had never been put in the same frame. They now are, in
`Reports/baseline_reproduction_table.md`, which grew from three columns to five and from 44 rows to
60. No retrain: every number comes from records already on disk.

**How we got there. Five checks, in order, each one a possible stop.**

1. **Are the LDO3 records complete?** Yes. `diagnostics/ldo3_report.py --verify-only` reports 215 of
   215 artifacts present, no integrity problems. Canonical prefixes are `encoder_ldo3__*` (few-shot)
   and `encoder_ldo3_zeroshot__*`. Everything else in `results/lodo/` is a different fold structure.
2. **Can the existing pooling code read them?** Yes, unchanged. The LDO3 `__perorigin.npz` and
   `__pernode.npz` archives carry an identical key set to `results/single/`, so
   `paper_compare.encoder_pooled()` works on them as written. Simulated read-only first: 20 of 20
   cells reconstructed, zero guard failures, dengue subsample path included.
3. **Are the transfer numbers depressed by the trunk early-stop defect?** No, on the seed we can
   check. The defect is real: `train/lodo.py:56` defaults `patience=12`, the cosine schedule at
   `T_max=91000` never annealed, and every scored record predates the fix. But the full-budget rerun
   under `encoder_ldo3full__*` at seed 42, all three folds, is **bit-identical** to its truncated
   twin. `diagnostics/ldo3full_check.py` reports 10 of 10 identical on a sha256 over every numeric
   field. The best checkpoints landed at steps 3000, 4000 and 7000, and patience would not have
   fired until 15k to 19k.
4. **Which cells can carry all three?** 16 of 20. Dengue plus the three influenza panels.
   **COVID has no baseline of any kind**, so the covid-out fold has no comparator and gets four
   explicit `(none)` rows rather than blanks. On dengue the only comparator is EpiGNN, since MTGNN
   stays excluded.
5. **Does the table check out?** `diagnostics/verify_paper_table.py` now pools the transfer and
   encoder columns from the per-**node** archives while the generator builds them from the
   per-**origin** sufficient statistics, so the two routes are independent. Verified, and `--mutate`
   caught 25 of 25 planted errors.

**What it says.**

Against our own single-disease ceiling, few-shot transfer is better in **4 of 16** cells and worse in
12, spanning -5.9 to +40.4 percent. Zero-shot is worse in **16 of 16**, +1.4 to +53.3 percent, and on
the four COVID cells by up to +204.6 percent. The cost grows with horizon on all three influenza
panels, which is the gradient LDO3 already showed.

**The one result that is easy to misread.** Against the best baseline in each cell, few-shot appears
to win 5 of 16 and zero-shot 3 of 16. Four of those five and all three of those are **dengue**, and
they do not survive the floors. Pooled on the same 2,392 nodes, a flat train-mean forecast scores
**484.5 / 483.4 / 480.2 / 477.0** at h3/h5/h10/h15. Few-shot transfer scores 435.1 / 467.4 / 482.3 /
482.5, so at h10 and h15 it loses to a flat line. EpiGNN loses to it too, at 500.1 and 504.7.
Persistence is 234.7 / 350.1 / 560.7 / 645.9, and **no dengue horizon clears both floors at once**.
So the apparent wins are dengue being a panel where the whole field loses to a constant, not transfer
skill. This is in the document and the verifier fails if it ever flips.

**Two figures.** `figures/baselines_vs_single.png` is the four baselines against our single-disease
encoder. `figures/transfer_vs_baselines.png` adds both transfer arms. Both are cell-pooled RMSE
normalised per row so our single-disease encoder is 1.0, because absolute RMSE spans an order of
magnitude across panels. Generator is `baseline_bars_figure.py`, PDFs alongside.

**Downsides and limits, stated.**
- The trunk control is **seed 42 only**, 1 of 5. It says no measured effect on the folds we checked,
  not proof for all 25 records. That caveat is printed on the figure.
- **This is not a race and the figure has to say so.** EpiGNN, Cola-GNN and HeatGNN are transductive
  and node-indexed, so they cannot be run in the held-out setting at all. The transfer bars are the
  price of the setting, not a head-to-head. In the figure they are hatched, in a separate colour
  family, and the subtitle states it.
- Everything here is **cell-pooled** RMSE. It must never be mixed with the country-macro Ebola
  headline numbers.
- Dengue is the 2,392-node subsample on every column, and every dengue row is marked.
- Still untouched: the MTGNN sentence at `Reports/Week4_Experiments_Stakeholder_Brief.md:172-174`.
