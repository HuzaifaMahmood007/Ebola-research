# A Menu of Disease-Agnostic Shared Spatio-Temporal Encoders for Cross-Disease Epidemic Forecasting with Few-Shot Transfer

## TL;DR
- The strongest candidate to prototype first is a **temporal-per-node encoder (dilated TCN or GRU) fused with strictly inductive spatial message passing (GraphSAGE/GIN/symmetric-normalised GCN with self-loops added before normalisation)** in a factorised T-then-S design: it is the only family that is size-agnostic by construction, needs no node identities, has a small shared trunk friendly to MAML/adapters, and — per our review — represents a genuine, unfilled gap in the epidemic literature.
- The popular "adaptive-graph" STGNNs (**Graph WaveNet, MTGNN, AGCRN**) must be **rejected for the shared trunk**: their learned per-node embedding dictionaries (E ∈ ℝ^{N×d}) are transductive and hard-wire a fixed node count, breaking cross-graph transfer; the leading epidemic GNNs (**EpiGNN, Cola-GNN**) are similarly size-locked (EpiGNN provably so via a learnable N×N matrix).
- The closest prior art — **CAPE (2025) and PEMS (ICLR 2024)** — proves cross-disease pre-training works, but both are **purely univariate/temporal with no graph encoder**; this project's contribution is precisely to add an inductive spatial channel that they lack, so they should be treated as strong baselines and inspiration rather than drop-in encoders.

## Key Findings
1. **Transductive node embeddings are disqualifying.** Graph WaveNet's self-adaptive adjacency is built from two learnable node-embedding dictionaries E1, E2 ∈ ℝ^{N×c}; MTGNN uses the same device; AGCRN's Node Adaptive Parameter Learning and Data Adaptive Graph Generation both key off a per-node embedding E ∈ ℝ^{N×d}. These parameters are indexed by node and fixed in size, so a model trained on 47 prefectures cannot be applied unchanged to 61 districts or 7,165 dengue nodes, and the embeddings would also leak disease identity — violating constraints 1 and 2.
2. **Inductivity comes from parameter-sharing over local neighbourhoods, not from node lookups.** GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., NeurIPS 2017), GIN, and vanilla symmetric-normalised GCN share aggregation weights across all nodes and generalise to unseen nodes/graph sizes; these are the natural spatial mixers for a size-agnostic trunk. Theory on GNN "size transferability" (via graphons/graphops) supports carry-over across graphs of different sizes when the local operator is shared.
3. **The leading epidemic-specific GNNs are size-locked.** EpiGNN's Region-Aware Graph Learner contains a learnable matrix W^s ∈ ℝ^{N×N} (plus degree vectors ∈ ℝ^N), which hard-fixes region count; Cola-GNN's location-aware attention parameters are themselves N-independent (additive attention over RNN hidden states), but the whole pipeline assumes a single fixed geographic adjacency and was never demonstrated across differently-sized graphs. Their disease-specific machinery (transmission-risk encoding, AR components) is not needed for a shared trunk.
4. **The closest transferable prior art is temporal-only.** CAPE (Zewen Liu, Juntong Ni, Bohan Wang, Max S. Y. Lau, Wei Jin, Emory University; arXiv:2502.03393, 2025 — retitled in v6, 23 Feb 2026, "CAPE: Covariate-Adjusted Pre-Training for Generalized Epidemic Time Series Forecasting," still a preprint under OpenReview review, forum 5veGth37O2) is a patch-based univariate autoregressive transformer with "compartmental prototypes"; its abstract reports "On a comprehensive benchmark spanning 17 diseases, CAPE significantly outperforms strong baselines with zero-shot forecasting," and the 17 diseases are described as "12 respiratory and 5 nonrespiratory." It has no graph/spatial module and has open-source code. PEMS (Kamarthi & Prakash, Georgia Tech; ICLR 2024, arXiv:2311.07841) is a 6-layer/8-head patch transformer pretrained with 4 self-supervised masking tasks on 11 named diseases ("Hepatitis A, measles, mumps, pertussis, polio, rubella, smallpox, diphtheria, influenza, typhoid and Cryptosporidiosis"); it reports "an 11–24% improvement in performance over previous state-of-the-art baselines and SSL methods" and transfers to unseen COVID-19 — but again univariate with no graph encoder.
5. **Cross-graph transfer via meta-learning already has an epidemic precedent.** Panagopoulos, Nikolentzos & Vazirgiannis ("Transfer Graph Neural Networks for Pandemic Forecasting," AAAI 2021, 35(6):4838–4845, arXiv:2009.08388) use an MPNN+LSTM over regional mobility graphs and transfer across 4 European countries (different graphs) using first-order MAML — direct evidence that an inductive message-passing trunk plus episodic meta-learning transfers across disjoint geographies.
6. **Spatio-temporal foundation models exist but are grid/urban-centric.** UniST (Yuan, Ding, Feng, Jin, Li, Tsinghua University; KDD '24, arXiv:2402.11838) uses masked pre-training + prompting, is tested on "more than 20 spatio-temporal scenarios," and in few-shot (1%/5% data) yields "an average improvement of 10.1%" over the best per-dataset baseline — but is built for urban grids and requires spatial pre-training. Time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, MOMENT) are univariate; Kalahasti et al. (Sorbonne/INSERM Institut Pierre Louis; medRxiv 2025.02.24.25322795, published as *Epidemics* 2026;55:100916) tested "five models across pathogens and locations, including influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), chickenpox, dengue," finding "Foundation models demonstrated strong accuracy in short-term forecasts and predicted multiple epidemic waves" — but none carry a graph inductive bias.

## Details

### Framing: what the constraints demand of the encoder
The seven hard constraints collapse into three architectural filters that eliminate most of the field:
- **Filter A — no node identities / size-agnostic (constraints 1, 2):** the shared weights may not contain any parameter indexed by node or sized by N. This immediately disqualifies transductive adaptive-graph learners.
- **Filter B — direct multi-horizon, factorisable, cheap on 8 GB at 7,165 nodes (constraints 3, 6):** favours factorised temporal-then-spatial designs with a direct multi-head output, and penalises anything with O(N²) dense spatial attention or per-node recurrence at the dengue scale.
- **Filter C — few-shot/meta-learning + conformal + SHAP friendliness (constraint 5):** favours a small shared trunk with a light adaptation surface (FiLM/adapter/head), stable latent features, and no reliance on a held-out calibration split.

Below is the menu of eight families, each assessed against these filters and the 7 constraints.

---

### Option 1 — Inductive temporal-per-node encoder + inductive spatial message passing (RECOMMENDED #1)
**Design.** Apply a shared per-node temporal encoder — a dilated causal TCN (WaveNet/Graph-WaveNet-style gated dilated convolutions) or a small GRU — independently to each node's 4-channel lookback window (w=20). Then mix spatially with an inductive GNN layer: symmetric-normalised GCN with self-identity added *before* degree normalisation (Â = D̃^{-1/2}(A+I)D̃^{-1/2}), GraphSAGE mean/pool aggregation, or GIN. Fusion is factorised **T-then-S** (or interleaved TCN→GCN blocks à la STGCN). A direct multi-horizon head emits h ∈ {3,5,10,15} in one shot (no rollout).

**Assessment.** Fully inductive and size-agnostic (shared weights over local neighbourhoods; self-loop-before-normalisation handles isolated influenza nodes without divide-by-zero). No node identities → disease-agnostic by construction. Low parameter count → trains on 8 GB and scales to 7,165 nodes with sparse message passing and neighbour sampling. Small trunk is ideal for MAML inner loops and FiLM/adapter heads. Latent per-node embeddings are clean inputs for SHAP; residuals feed online adaptive conformal inference directly. Cost of giving up learned adjacency: some accuracy loss versus adaptive-graph models on any single in-distribution dataset, but this is exactly the price of transferability. **Evidence base:** Panagopoulos et al. (AAAI 2021) is the direct precedent — an inductive MPNN trunk plus MAML transferring across countries' graphs. **Public code:** GCN/GraphSAGE/GIN in PyTorch Geometric; TCN blocks from Graph WaveNet's repo; STGCN reference implementations widely available.

### Option 2 — Classic factorised STGCN-style GCN+gated-TCN (RECOMMENDED #2 / strong baseline)
**Design.** STGCN (Yu et al., IJCAI 2018): two spatio-temporal conv blocks, each a Chebyshev/1st-order graph conv sandwiched between gated 1-D temporal convolutions, fully convolutional, fast, few parameters. Uses a *provided* graph (A_geo, optionally A_mob).

**Assessment.** Inductive as long as the graph convolution uses a fixed polynomial order and shared filters (no node embeddings) — STGCN qualifies. Size-agnostic; no node identities. Extremely efficient (the survey literature repeatedly notes STGCN is the most compute-efficient of the classic STGNNs but somewhat lower-accuracy than attention/adaptive models). Direct multi-horizon output natural. Good meta-learning/adapter surface. The main limitation: purely predefined graph, so it cannot discover latent spatial dependency — acceptable here because latent-graph learning is what breaks transfer. **Public code available.**

### Option 3 — DCRNN-style diffusion + recurrence (conditional)
**Design.** DCRNN (Li et al., ICLR 2018): bidirectional diffusion graph convolution embedded in GRU cells, seq2seq encoder–decoder. Diffusion convolution uses shared transition-matrix powers — no node embeddings — so the *core* operator is inductive.

**Assessment.** Inductive and size-agnostic in principle (shared diffusion filters). But: (a) the seq2seq decoder is autoregressive with scheduled sampling, conflicting with the "direct multi-horizon, no rollout" constraint (needs surgery to a direct multi-head decoder); (b) recurrent rollout is slow and memory-heavy — problematic at 7,165 nodes on 8 GB; (c) it is repeatedly reported as the slowest of the classic baselines (GMAN's authors note DCRNN "runs much slower than other methods due to the time-consuming sequence learning in recurrent networks"). Usable but not first-choice; keep as an ablation.

### Option 4 — Adaptive-graph STGNNs: Graph WaveNet / MTGNN / AGCRN (REJECT for shared trunk)
**Design.** Graph WaveNet (Wu et al., IJCAI 2019) = dilated gated TCN + diffusion conv augmented by a self-adaptive adjacency Â = softmax(ReLU(E1E2^T)), E1,E2 ∈ ℝ^{N×c}. MTGNN reuses the node-embedding graph learner; AGCRN (Bai et al., NeurIPS 2020) adds Node Adaptive Parameter Learning + Data Adaptive Graph Generation, both keyed on E ∈ ℝ^{N×d}.

**Assessment.** These are state of the art on single fixed-node datasets, but the learned node embeddings are **transductive**: parameters are indexed by node and sized by N, so the shared weights cannot move to a graph of different size, and the embeddings would encode disease/geographic identity (leakage). A NeurIPS reviewer explicitly flagged AGCRN's O(N²) computation and asked how it would scale to large graphs — a direct concern for the 7,165-node dengue case. Reject the adaptive-adjacency module for the shared encoder. *Salvageable pattern:* Graph WaveNet's gated-dilated-TCN temporal block and the diffusion-conv-with-self-loops (minus the adaptive term) are exactly the inductive ingredients of Option 1. A static-only or attention-derived (data-dependent, not parameter-indexed) adjacency is the inductive escape hatch.

### Option 5 — Epidemic-specific GNNs: EpiGNN / Cola-GNN / STAN / MepoGNN / CausalGNN (REJECT trunk; borrow ideas)
**Design.** Cola-GNN (Deng et al., CIKM 2020): RNN + cross-location attention + dilated conv + graph message passing for long-horizon ILI. EpiGNN (Xie et al., PRICAI 2022, arXiv:2208.11517): multi-scale CNN + GCN + transmission-risk encoding + Region-Aware Graph Learner. STAN, MepoGNN (Cao et al., ECML PKDD 2022), CausalGNN (Wang et al., AAAI 2022) embed SIR/SIRD/metapopulation mechanistic priors into the GNN.

**Assessment.** EpiGNN is provably size-locked (learnable W^s ∈ ℝ^{N×N}, degree vectors ∈ ℝ^N). Cola-GNN's additive-attention parameters are N-independent (a genuinely inductive attention motif worth borrowing), but the pipeline assumes a single fixed adjacency and was never shown across sizes. STAN forecasts a single region ("it can only predict infections of a single region"); MepoGNN/CausalGNN bake in disease-specific compartmental parameters that violate disease-agnosticism if placed in the shared trunk. **Verdict:** none serve as the shared trunk unchanged; Cola-GNN's location-aware attention and EpiGNN's multi-scale temporal convolutions are transferable *motifs*. Note the recent EpiHybridGNN (arXiv:2511.15469) confirms both EpiGNN and Cola-GNN operate over static/fixed region-level graphs.

### Option 6 — Spatio-temporal transformers: GMAN / STTN / ASTGNN / graph transformers (conditional, cost-sensitive)
**Design.** GMAN (Zheng et al., AAAI 2020): encoder–decoder of stacked spatial + temporal attention blocks with a transform-attention bridge (naturally direct multi-horizon, avoiding rollout). STTN/ASTGNN interleave spatial and temporal self-attention.

**Assessment.** Attention weights can be made inductive (data-dependent, not node-indexed) — attractive. GMAN's transform-attention decoder directly aligns with the direct-multi-horizon requirement and "alleviate[s] the error propagation problem among prediction time steps." But joint/separated spatio-temporal attention is O(TN²) or O(NT²); at N≈7,165 this is prohibitive on 8 GB without linear-attention or agent/inducing-point approximations, which degrade sharpness (as the STAGformer and STGformer efficiency literature documents). Many ST-transformers also inject learned spatio-temporal *positional/adaptive embeddings* that can be node-indexed (transductive) — must be audited. **Verdict:** viable for the small graphs (10–61 nodes), risky for dengue; keep as a higher-capacity variant with efficiency guards.

### Option 7 — Pre-trained / foundation ST & time-series encoders: UniST, TSFMs (Chronos/TimesFM/Moirai/MOMENT) (baseline / auxiliary)
**Design.** UniST (KDD 2024): masked spatio-temporal pre-training + prompt/meta-adapter, strong few-shot/zero-shot on 20+ urban scenarios (10.1% average few-shot improvement). TSFMs: patch or token transformers pretrained on huge corpora, used zero-shot or with LoRA/PEFT (Chronos tokenises via a T5 vocabulary; TimesFM/Moirai/MOMENT use continuous patches; Moirai natively supports covariates).

**Assessment.** UniST is grid/urban-oriented and needs ST pre-training; not disease-agnostic out of the box and not obviously size-agnostic on arbitrary sparse epidemic graphs. TSFMs are univariate (no graph inductive bias), but are directly relevant: a frozen/lightly-adapted TSFM (or its architecture) is a legitimate **per-node temporal encoder** inside Option 1, and Kalahasti et al. (*Epidemics* 2026) show TSFMs forecast influenza/RSV/chickenpox/dengue competitively in data-scarce settings. **Verdict:** use a small TSFM (or its architecture) as the temporal module and/or as a zero-shot baseline; do not adopt a large frozen foundation model as the whole trunk (8 GB, SHAP transparency, and few-shot inner-loop constraints all argue against it).

### Option 8 — Epidemic pre-trained temporal models: CAPE / PEMS (closest prior art; baselines + design template)
**Design.** CAPE (arXiv:2502.03393, 2025): patch-based autoregressive transformer with a learned dictionary of "compartmental prototypes," pretrained on simulated epidemics (the EpiRecipe pipeline of 12 compartments), zero-shot on 17 diseases, open source (github.com/nuuuh/CAPE). PEMS (ICLR 2024): 6-layer/8-head patch transformer, 4 self-supervised masking tasks (RANDMASK/LASTMASK/PEAKMASK/SEASONDETECT), 11-disease pretraining, transfers to unseen COVID-19.

**Assessment.** These prove the central thesis (cross-disease pre-training + few-shot transfer works) but are **purely temporal/univariate with no spatial encoder** — the exact gap this project fills. Treat as (a) must-cite closest prior art, (b) strong non-graph baselines, and (c) a template for the self-supervised pre-training and disease-agnostic masking that should sit on top of the inductive spatial trunk.

### Cross-cutting pitfalls to design around
- **Node-indexed parameters leak and break transfer.** Audit every candidate for any parameter sized by N (adjacency-as-parameter, node embeddings, positional embeddings). Prefer data-dependent attention or shared local filters.
- **Normalisation/scaling interacts with transfer.** Because incidence scale differs by disease and node count, per-window instance normalisation (RevIN-style, as PEMS uses) and the mandated normalised-incidence channel are important; the self-loop-before-degree-normalisation rule is essential for isolated nodes.
- **Size generalisation is theoretically supported but empirically fragile.** GNN transferability results (Levie et al.; graphop-based bounds) assume structurally similar graphs; disjoint geographies with different densities (10 vs 7,165 nodes) stress this. Mitigate with spatial masking / forecasting-unobserved-node-states training (random node masking during pre-training, per Roth et al.) to force size-robust representations.
- **Conformal UQ fit.** Online adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs & Candès ACI; conformal PID control; SPCI) needs no held-out calibration set and wraps any forecaster — compatible with all options; feature-space online conformal is easier with a small stable trunk (Options 1–2).
- **SHAP fit.** Global+local attribution is cleanest on a low-dimensional, stable latent (Options 1–2); expensive/unstable on large attention stacks (Option 6) and opaque on large frozen foundation models (Option 7).

## Recommendations
1. **Prototype first (Option 1): inductive TCN/GRU-per-node + inductive spatial mixing (GCN-with-self-loops / GraphSAGE / GIN), factorised T-then-S, direct multi-horizon head.** It uniquely satisfies all seven constraints and fills a literature gap (no epidemic paper adopts GraphSAGE/GIN-style inductive size-agnostic message passing for cross-graph/cross-disease transfer). Build the shared trunk small; expose adaptation via FiLM/adapters + a light per-disease head for MAML/leave-one-disease-out; wrap with online ACI; attribute with SHAP on the latent.
2. **Prototype second (Option 2): STGCN-style GCN+gated-TCN** as the efficient, well-understood, fully-inductive comparator. If STGCN matches Option 1, prefer it for simplicity; if Option 1 wins, the delta quantifies the value of the flexible temporal encoder.
3. **Third (Option 6, small-graph variant): a GMAN-style spatio-temporal transformer with inductive (non-node-indexed) attention and a direct-multi-horizon transform-attention decoder**, run only where N is small (10–61), with linear-attention or inducing-point guards before attempting the dengue graph.
4. **Always include as baselines:** CAPE and PEMS (temporal-only pre-trained), a TSFM zero-shot (Chronos/TimesFM), and the MPNN+MAML transfer recipe (Panagopoulos). **Always include as ablations/negative controls:** Graph WaveNet/AGCRN retrained per-graph (to demonstrate adaptive graphs win in-distribution but cannot transfer weights across sizes).
5. **Decision thresholds / benchmarks that change the ranking:** (a) if inductive spatial mixing on the 7,165-node dengue graph OOMs at 8 GB even with neighbour sampling, fall back to Option 2 with sparse Chebyshev order K≤2; (b) if leave-one-disease-out zero-shot for Ebola (~52/61 districts) shows the spatial channel adds <2–3% over a temporal-only PEMS/CAPE baseline, demote spatial mixing to a light residual and lead with the temporal trunk; (c) if online ACI coverage drifts >±5% from nominal under cross-disease shift, add conformal-PID control; (d) if SHAP attributions are unstable across seeds, prefer the smallest trunk (Option 2).

## Caveats
- CAPE is currently a preprint (arXiv:2502.03393; retitled in v6 "CAPE: Covariate-Adjusted Pre-Training for Generalized Epidemic Time Series Forecasting," under OpenReview review, forum 5veGth37O2). Its precise zero-shot win margins live in tables/appendix; the abstract gives only qualitative claims. Its "17 diseases" is the *evaluation* set (12 respiratory + 5 nonrespiratory); pretraining is largely on simulated trajectories plus real data.
- "Inductive" for STGCN/DCRNN holds only if implemented without node-indexed parameters; some published variants add adaptive/node-embedding terms that silently reintroduce transductivity — verify the specific implementation before adopting.
- GNN size-transferability theory (graphons/graphops) provides asymptotic guarantees for structurally similar graphs; it does not guarantee good empirical transfer across the extreme size range (10→7,165) and disjoint geographies here — this must be validated, not assumed.
- Foundation-model claims (UniST's 10.1% few-shot improvement; TSFM zero-shot epidemic accuracy in Kalahasti et al.) come from a peer-reviewed article and a KDD paper respectively, but generalisation to this project's specific diseases/graphs is unproven — re-benchmark on the project's own data.
- Public code exists for STGCN, DCRNN, Graph WaveNet, MTGNN, AGCRN, GMAN, Cola-GNN, EpiGNN, UniST, CAPE, and the major TSFMs, but licences, maintenance, and exact reproducibility vary; the EpiLearn library (Emory-Melody) and the Awesome-GNN4TS (TPAMI 2024) resource list are useful for standardised implementations and baselines.
